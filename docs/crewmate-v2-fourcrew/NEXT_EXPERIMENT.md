# Proposed next four-crew learning study — not authorized to run

Goal: determine whether shared-policy learning improves legitimate reporting and evidence-conditioned team decisions, beyond random initialization and reporter identity. Exactly four learned crewmates versus one scripted impostor. No final-test partition, learned impostor or population expansion.

1. **Matched models:** independent fresh initialization 17 for the new four-crew original control and shaped-v2 (+1.5 report) arm. Same architecture, repaired mechanics, scenarios, scripted-impostor mixture, workers, PPO coefficients and checkpoint-selection rule. Both new controls have zero individual ghost-task reward. Keep a frozen zero-update initialization as a third evaluation reference. Do not reuse focal pilot checkpoints as if they were four-crew models.
2. **Small budget:** target about **2,048 individual actor choices per trained arm**, roughly 512 per actor only if exposure is balanced. Collect complete matched-scenario batches and register an explicit final-batch overshoot tolerance before approval; report actual choices per actor and match count. Current collector cannot promise an exact decision cap within a partially completed game. Use a fixed registered final collection budget, never best-of-validation tuning.
3. **Evidence availability:** ordinary games plus clearly labeled evidence-opportunity scenarios. Use legal simulated prefixes and actor-local history to provide actual body visibility/reporting choices, not hidden-role-based curriculum selection or fabricated evidence. Do not force the trainable crew to report or vote. Replaying a prefix must rebuild each actor's legitimate memory independently. Register minimum opportunity coverage and stop as inconclusive if it is absent.
4. **Reporter controls:** eight fresh paired development scenarios, each with an ordinary and a controlled meeting/evidence context: 16 games per policy, **48 executions for three policies / eight scenario clusters**. Balance innocent/impostor reporter contexts using legitimate scripted prefixes as a separate stress test, while keeping natural ordinary-game results separate. Prefix-generated meetings do not count as learned reports. Numeric seed ranges, exact prefixes and the runner extension require registration before execution; no proposed seeds have been opened here.
5. **Outcomes:** report team win reason, actor task/ghost contribution, reporting opportunities and acceptance, votes/skips/incorrect accusations, survival/isolation, public/direct evidence support, per-actor suspicion calibration, reporter-only baseline, reporter-role strata and paired cluster uncertainty. Track task-only wins and repeated ballot credit separately. One shared frozen belief predictor remains distinct from the shared PPO actor policy.
6. **Compute estimate:** the 60-second engineering match took about 16–19 wall seconds for 32 actor choices; four 3-second matches plus a PPO update took 12.85s including worker startup. These are poor predictors of full 900-second matches and long meetings. A rough serial training projection is about **40 minutes for 4,096 choices across both arms**, before batch overshoot/evaluation. Sixteen validation games for each of three policies could dominate runtime. Plan **2–4 hours total with a proposed four-hour hard stop**, preserving partial artifacts and marking incomplete evaluations honestly. This is a planning range, not a measured four-worker speedup. If approval requires a tighter ceiling, reduce the registered study before launch rather than silently shortening it later.

Preparation still needed before this prospective study is reviewable for launch: finalize exact scenario prefixes/partitions and opportunity quotas, complete evaluation/approval registration, choose complete-match overshoot rules and measure a bounded representative evaluation-cost check. These are part of study registration, not permission to start a full pilot now. No reward retuning is recommended until meaningful reporting opportunities exist; a different wrong-vote penalty would be a separate hypothesis.
